# Numeric Discovery Brief

_Pre-engagement analysis based on Strata wiki patterns_

## Company Overview
- **Company:** Numeric (numeric.io)
- **Stage:** Series B, $51M raised
- **Product:** AI-native accounting automation platform
- **Origin:** Month-end close management (reconciliations, flux analysis, journal entries)
- **Expansion:** Full financial data layer -- CFO and finance capabilities bridging accounting operations and finance intelligence
- **Primary buyer:** Controllers and accounting teams at high-growth SaaS companies, expanding toward CFOs
- **Competitors:** BlackLine and FloQast (close management), QuickBooks and NetSuite (finance/ERP)

---

## 1. Recommended Positioning Stance: Category Creation

**Category creation**, executed in the TeamOhana style.

TeamOhana carved out "Headcount Management Platform" in the gap between FP&A tools (not built for recruiting) and HRIS platforms (not built for finance). Numeric's situation is nearly identical in shape: close management tools (BlackLine, FloQast) aren't built for finance intelligence, and ERP/GL systems (QuickBooks, NetSuite) aren't built for accounting operations. The category lives in the gap.

Critical nuance from the wiki's category trap pattern: BrightEdge and Unity Industry both struggled with being pigeonholed by their origin category ("SEO tool," "game engine for industry"). Numeric faces the same risk -- buyers currently know them as a close management tool. The category creation play has to pull them out of the close management box, not just add features to it. The new category name cannot reference "close" at all.

**Working category name candidates (to validate in discovery):**
- Accounting Intelligence Platform
- Financial Operations Platform
- AI Accounting Platform

## 2. Most Analogous Hero Problems

### Primary analog: TeamOhana's "Headcount Headache"
- Both name a universal, operational pain that everyone tolerates because the tools never existed to fix it
- Both frame the problem as a process failure across teams, not a product gap -- headcount spans Finance/HR/Talent; Numeric's problem spans accounting operations/financial reporting/CFO strategy
- Both quantify hidden costs (TeamOhana: $294K in unapproved headcount; Numeric should find its equivalent -- errors in reconciliations, hours burned on manual close, delayed reporting cycles)

### Secondary analog: Eudia's "Speed-to-risk ratio"
For Controllers, there's a similar tension: speed-to-close vs. accuracy. Every close cycle forces a trade-off between getting the books done fast and getting them done right. AI changes that equation the same way it changed Eudia's speed-to-risk ratio for legal teams.

### Hero problem candidates to test in discovery
- **"The Accounting Gap"** -- the gap between closing the books and understanding them (operational framing, like "The Reality Gap")
- **"The Close Ceiling"** -- you can optimize the close all you want, but without the financial intelligence layer, you're just getting to wrong answers faster (paradox framing, like "The Customer Value Paradox")
- **"The Finance Blind Spot"** -- Controllers have the data but not the tools to make it strategic; CFOs have the mandate but not the operational detail (gap framing, like TeamOhana's cross-functional dynamic)

## 3. Recommended Opening Strategies

Layer two moves (the pattern for category creators):

### Primary: Surface a hidden cost (TeamOhana model)
Quantify what accounting teams are losing: hours per close cycle, error rates in manual reconciliations, delayed financial insights that cost the business real money. Controllers respond to quantified waste -- maps directly to the wiki's finding that "surface a hidden cost works best for operational buyers (VP Ops, CFO)."

### Secondary: Name a paradox
Something like: "The more tools you add to your accounting stack, the less your finance team actually understands." Or: "You've optimized the close to 5 days, but your CFO still can't answer basic questions about the business." The paradox is that close management success doesn't translate to financial intelligence -- speed without sight.

### Tertiary: Invoke an inevitable future
AI is collapsing the wall between accounting operations and financial intelligence. Teams that treat these as separate functions will be left behind. Same move Eudia and Agency use to close their openings.

Avoid "reframe the market" as the primary move -- that works for category redefininers (BrightEdge, Unity Industry) who already own a category.

## 4. Discovery Call Listening Guide

### From Controllers (primary buyer)
- The language they use for the gap between "closing the books" and "understanding the business." Do they feel this gap? What do they call it?
- Frustration with tools that only do half the job -- listen for: "BlackLine/FloQast is great for close, but..." or "We still end up in spreadsheets for..."
- The emotional weight of the close cycle -- is it dread, exhaustion, anxiety about errors? (Emotional language maps to hero problem naming)
- How they describe their relationship with the CFO -- do they feel like data janitors vs. strategic partners? This tension could power the hero problem

### From CFOs (expansion buyer)
- Whether they see accounting as a cost center or a strategic function -- and what would change that
- What questions they can't answer today because the data is trapped in close management tools or ERP systems
- Their reaction to the phrase "financial intelligence" or "accounting intelligence" -- does it resonate or feel like jargon?

### Trigger events to probe for
- Post-IPO or pre-IPO readiness (audit pressure, SOX compliance)
- Rapid growth that's outpacing the accounting team's capacity (like TeamOhana's headcount growth trigger)
- CFO hire or new Controller -- new executives often trigger tool consolidation
- Failed ERP migration or ERP frustration (the moment they realize NetSuite won't solve the problem)

### Cross-functional dynamic (critical -- mirrors TeamOhana)
- How do Controllers and CFOs communicate today? Is there a translation layer?
- TeamOhana's key insight was that headcount tools fail when they over-prioritize one persona
- Numeric must serve both Controllers (operational) and CFOs (strategic) without alienating either

## 5. Category Trap to Avoid

The wiki identifies this pattern explicitly: "Avoid the category trap -- several clients needed to avoid being pigeonholed by an outdated category label."

**Numeric's specific trap: being seen as "FloQast/BlackLine + AI" or "close management with extras."** Same trap BrightEdge faced ("SEO tool") and Unity Industry faced ("game engine for industry").

### Tactical implications
- **Don't lead with close management in the narrative.** If the opening mentions month-end close, the buyer's brain slots Numeric into the close management category and everything after gets filtered through that lens
- **Lead with the gap, not the origin.** TeamOhana didn't open with "we started as a headcount spreadsheet replacement." They opened with "70% of your OPEX is managed on disconnected tools." Numeric should open with the problem (the gap between accounting operations and financial intelligence) not the product history
- **The differentiation table needs three axes** (TeamOhana's pattern):
  1. vs. close management tools (BlackLine/FloQast -- only close, no intelligence)
  2. vs. ERP/GL (QuickBooks/NetSuite -- too broad, not purpose-built for the accounting-to-finance workflow)
  3. vs. spreadsheets/manual processes (the status quo)
- **The named enemy should be the architecture, not the competitors.** Agency's enemy was "bolt-on AI," Eudia's was "artificial intelligence" (off-the-shelf), TeamOhana's was "spreadsheets and disconnected tools." Numeric's enemy should be something like "the disconnected accounting stack" or "the close-to-finance gap" -- the structural problem, not BlackLine specifically
