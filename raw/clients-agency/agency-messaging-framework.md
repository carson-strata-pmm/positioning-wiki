Chief Revenue Officer
Decision Maker
Mid-market
A Chief Revenue Officer (CRO) is a senior executive responsible for driving all revenue-generating activities within a company. This broad role typically encompasses the oversight and strategic alignment of sales, marketing, and often customer success teams. The CRO's primary goal is to ensure sustainable revenue growth by developing and executing comprehensive strategies, optimizing pricing, identifying new market opportunities, and improving the overall customer journey. They are highly data-driven, constantly analyzing performance metrics to refine processes and maximize profitability across the organization.
Chief Customer Officer 
Decision Maker
Enterprise 
A Chief Customer Officer (CCO) is responsible for the entire post-sale customer experience, focusing on long-term customer value and retention. This role typically oversees customer-facing teams such as Customer Success, Support, and Professional Services. The CCO is accountable for gross and net revenue retention (GRR and NRR) and acts as the internal advocate for the customer, driving a customer-first culture throughout the organization. While compensation may differ from sales roles, the CCO plays a crucial part in identifying expansion opportunities and monitoring customer health and risk.

The Bureaucracy Bottleneck
Customer Value Paradox

This is the irony with which we currently run our businesses. We reflexively hire more people as we grow to serve our customers. But the truth is, the more people and resources you invest in delivering customer value, the more challenging it becomes, jeopardizing renewals and limiting expansion. 



Pain Pillars
Limited customer coverage
Terrible customer experience

Pain Statement
You can only serve a fraction of your accounts and are unable to touch every customer.
Fragmented engagement, low-value touches, and delayed responses frustrate customers.
Customers don’t achieve their desired outcomes. 
Causes 
Headcount cost: Scaling customer success and support through people is prohibitively expensive and unsustainable.

Humans have limitations: People simply can’t absorb all the data needed to truly understand every customer or respond quickly enough to meet their needs. 
Too many people touch the customer: Every handoff between teams creates friction, delays, and customer frustration.

Low-value customer interactions: It’s too hard to determine the highest-value action for every customer at any given moment, so customers are flooded with generic ‘just checking in’ messages that waste time and erode trust.
Poor product adoption: Teams lack the bandwidth or tools to spot adoption signals and act quickly across the entire base.
You don’t know the customer: Disconnected data traps critical insights like usage and sentiment, keeping you from personalizing experiences that drive adoption of the features most valuable to your customers. 

Negative Consequences

 
Churn: Poor engagement across your biggest accounts and the long tail leaves customers frustrated and disconnected, putting renewals at risk across your entire business. 
Lost expansion opportunities: Sales and success teams lose opportunities for cross-selling and upselling.
No advocacy: Many customers feel forgotten or undervalued, which increases their frustration. They experience friction, delays, and inconsistency in support and success, eroding trust and satisfaction.
