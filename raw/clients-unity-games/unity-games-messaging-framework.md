UA leaders are responsible for finding the right players at the right cost. They operate in an increasingly opaque, post-IDFA (Identifier for Advertisers) world where performance signals are hidden inside platform walled gardens. Their job depends on understanding why campaigns work, scaling profitable channels, and continually producing creative assets that quickly become stale as others race to replicate anything that appears to be working. UA teams want more transparency, more control, and tools that help them identify high-value players sooner. They measure success by ensuring Cost Per Impression (CPI) stays below Lifetime Value (LTV), meaning every acquired player generates more revenue over time than it costs to acquire them, and by maintaining consistent performance across networks. They value partners who help them reduce uncertainty and improve efficiency.

You deserve better insights and more capable tools that help you reach the players who will actually value your game.

​​You should have the insight and automation to acquire the right players with confidence.

Monetization leaders are responsible for turning player engagement into sustainable revenue (i.e., premium games (paid), In-App Ads (IAA), In-App purchases (IAP), or a combination of them). High-level framing
The Success Gap
It’s never been a better time to make games, but making a successful game now requires more than ever. Finding, retaining, and monetizing an audience across multiple platforms takes developers away from the creative work that matters most – the game. Unity helps developers bridge that gap between their ambition and success by abstracting the complexities.


Problem Pillars
Platform requirements consume development resources 
Demand for live content and operations is higher than ever

It's harder than ever to find new players and monetize games
Problem Statement
Teams lose time they could invest in gameplay, design, and commercial strategy to performance optimization, platform requirements, and other technical constraints.
Running live games now requires constant updates, events, and operational work that stretch teams beyond their resources.
Discovery is fragmented across platforms, monetization rules differ by endpoint, and teams lack the insight they need to reach the players who will connect with their game.
Causes 
Fragmentation of platform requirements: Each platform has its own technical, policy, and content rules, requiring teams to build and maintain different implementations for core systems like achievements, saves, authentication, and store features.

Performance optimization across diverse hardware: Supporting thousands of devices/hardware combinations forces teams to spend significant time optimizing assets, debugging device-specific crashes, and managing memory budgets just to ensure stable performance.
 
Rising player expectations: Players now expect frequent updates, events, balance changes, and new content, turning every game into an ongoing service that requires constant production and iteration.

Limited access to player data: Teams struggle to see how players progress, where they churn, and which features succeed because analytics systems are fragmented or too technical for anyone except engineers and data scientists.

LiveOps tools and workflows are fragmented: Running a modern live game requires multiple disconnected tools for events, tuning, A/B tests, telemetry, and economy changes, creating operational friction and slowing teams down.

Crowded game market: With so many titles available and most players sticking to games that launched years ago, it’s extremely difficult for new releases to break through and get noticed.

Insights fragmented across platforms: Data lives across disconnected platforms, and walled gardens limit visibility into the signals that drive discovery and performance, making it hard to understand your audience or optimize growth.

Monetization rules and infrastructure differ across endpoints: Each platform has its own commerce rules, policies, formats, and technical requirements, making it difficult to build consistent monetization systems and optimize revenue across all endpoints.
Negative Consequences

Revenue, cost, risk
Creativity is constrained, and development slows down: Meeting platform, operational, and infrastructure demands take time away from exploring new ideas and extend production timelines.

LiveOps is forced to trade off between quality and scale: With limited resources and rising expectations, teams must decide between delivering more content or delivering better content.

Discovery is fragmented and inconsistent: Mobile developers have dedicated UA tooling, while PC, console, and XR teams rely on platform holders or publishers to surface their games, limiting their ability to reach the right audience directly.

Sustaining a successful game in the long term is beyond your control: rising acquisition costs, growing operational demands, and inconsistent monetization rules make long-term profitability harder to achieve.

Differentiation
Why are we different (and better) than the competition?

Vs. 3D editors

Vs. Ad networks 

AppLovin 
A complete ecosystem, not just an Engine: Unity offers a deeply extensible ecosystem with powerful first- and third-party tools that accelerate workflows and simplify non-creative tasks across a game’s lifecycle. Unreal also has a large ecosystem of plugins and tools, but these are primarily focused on creation. Unity’s ecosystem extends beyond the editor into deployment, operations, player insight, and monetization, giving teams support across the full lifecycle of their game.

Lower barrier to entry and build/iterate faster: Unity’s C# scripting, accessible editor, and flexible architecture make it easier for teams to onboard quickly and iterate often. C# also serves as a perfect bridge for AI code generation, enabling everything from gameplay to system code. Unreal’s Blueprints system is powerful and artist-friendly for visual logic, but developing deeper systems in C++ can introduce more complexity. Unity’s faster iteration cycles and lower entry threshold make it especially effective for teams targeting multiple platforms or moving quickly through prototyping and production.

A flexible philosophy that supports more platforms and development styles: Unity’s additive approach gives teams a lightweight starting point with the flexibility to layer in complexity as their game evolves. This makes Unity well-suited for a wide range of platforms, including mobile, PC, console, AR, VR, and web. Unreal follows a subtractive philosophy, providing high-fidelity features enabled by default, which is powerful for high-end titles but can require more work to optimize for other platforms. Unity’s design philosophy and broad platform support give developers greater control over performance, scope, and deployment targets.

Backed by the largest game development community in the world: Unity has one of the largest and most active game development communities, giving teams access to extensive shared knowledge, community-built tooling, and fast problem-solving resources. We are relied on by thousands of studios worldwide, including the largest mobile and cross-platform publishers, showing that we can support projects of any size and complexity. 
Full-stack for creation + live ops + data insights: AppLovin (and similar networks) focus on monetization, UA, or ad-network services only. They do not provide game creation, cross-platform build support, live ops infrastructure, or player data + analytics tied to the game engine itself. With Unity, developers get end-to-end support for building, deploying, running, and monetizing their games on a single integrated platform. This avoids “stitching together” separate tools, reduces friction, and gives unified data/insight across the game lifecycle.

More control over game success: Ad networks often impose business model constraints, revenue-share rules, and UA strategies that lock developers into short-term, ad-driven growth. Unity gives developers greater control over how they monetize across platforms, with flexible business models, player insights, and live ops tools that support more sustainable, long-term success.
