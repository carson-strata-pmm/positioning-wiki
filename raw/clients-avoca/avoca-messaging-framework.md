Owner Operator
Decision Maker
CEO, GM, President 
Owner-operators are responsible for the overall performance and growth of the business. They focus on revenue, profitability, and operational efficiency across the entire company. In service businesses, this role often balances strategic leadership with hands-on oversight of operations, marketing, and customer experience. 

Owner-operators are highly focused on capturing demand, maximizing technician productivity, and ensuring the business can scale without adding unnecessary overhead. They care about systems that improve performance, create visibility into what’s working, and allow the company to grow without requiring constant involvement in day-to-day decisions. Ultimately, their goal is to build a business that runs predictably and profitably.
Customer Service Leader
Decision Maker & Champion 
Director of Customer Services, Inside Sales Manager
Customer service leaders are responsible for managing the team that answers calls, books jobs, and handles customer communication. They oversee call center performance, staffing, scheduling, training, and quality assurance. Their success is measured by booking rates, response times, and the consistency of the customer experience. 

These leaders constantly work to improve call handling, coach agents, and ensure that every customer interaction leads to the best possible outcome. However, they often operate with limited visibility into performance across calls, follow-ups, and customer journeys. As a result, they are highly interested in tools that improve consistency, provide actionable insights, and help their teams capture more opportunities.
Digital Transformation
Decision Maker & Champion 
CTO,
Head of Digital Transformation 
Digital transformation leaders typically operate within private equity–backed service companies that manage multiple brands or regional businesses. Their mandate is to standardize operations and technology across the portfolio while supporting rapid acquisition and integration of new companies. 

They oversee the core systems that power customer experience and operations, including CRM platforms like ServiceTitan, communication tools, and back-office workflow automation. Their focus is on improving efficiency, creating consistent processes across locations, and unlocking data visibility at the platform level. They prioritize solutions that integrate with existing systems, scale across dozens of locations, and help transform fragmented local operations into a unified, high-performing platform.

And every miss is a lead handed straight to your competitors, and most operators don't see the full cost until growth hits a ceiling.

Staffing challenges
Problem Statement
When inbound demand isn’t captured, warm leads go cold, and there’s no proactive motion to create jobs, boards stay underfilled, and technician capacity goes unused.
Customer interactions vary widely depending on who answers the phone and how they handle the call.
Staffing the phones requires constant hiring, training, and management of a workforce that is difficult and expensive to maintain.
Causes 
Coverage limitations:
When teams are stretched thin, calls go unanswered, digital leads wait too long, and demand spikes overwhelm the front office. 

No proactive demand creation: There’s no consistent system for reactivating past customers, working on unsold estimates, or creating demand before idle capacity turns into lost revenue.

Slow, inconsistent follow-up:
Estimates, voicemail callbacks, and unscheduled jobs require consistent follow-up. In busy operations, these tasks get deprioritized, and warm leads sit too long or are forgotten entirely.


 
Individual CSR variability: Every CSR handles calls differently. Tone, qualification depth, urgency handling, and response speed vary shift to shift.

Limited visibility into CSR performance: Owners know results vary, but they can’t see where calls break down, which leads are mishandled, or what top performers do differently, making it hard to coach or improve outcomes. 


Hiring challenges:
It’s difficult to recruit and retain reliable, customer-facing employees for entry-level CSR roles, limiting the ability to consistently staff the front office. 

High turnover: CSR roles experience frequent churn, sending owners back into a never-ending hiring and training cycle.

Seasonal and other demand spikes: Call volume fluctuates with seasonality and peak periods, making it difficult to align staffing with demand without over- or under-resourcing.


Negative Consequences






Revenue, cost, risk
Lost revenue: Leads fall through the cracks, follow-ups are missed, and booking inefficiencies compound, so available demand never turns into booked jobs.

Limited growth: Scaling requires adding more people, making it difficult to grow revenue without increasing overhead and operational complexity.

Inability to improve performance: When performance drivers aren’t visible, owners/managers can’t identify who needs coaching, what script works, or where the process breaks down.

Underutilized technicians: Techs are sitting idle while demand exists. Gaps in the schedule mean you’re paying for capacity you’re not using, turning every missed opportunity into lost productivity and wasted labor.

Higher operating costs: Staffing phones requires hiring, training, and managing large CSR teams — often supplemented by expensive overflow call centers — without any guarantee of performance.

No or negative reviews: Customers don’t leave reviews — or worse, leave negative ones — hurting your reputation and reducing future demand.

Use Case

Inbound lead capture
A customer calls during a busy Monday morning. Your CSRs are on other lines, the phone rings six times, and goes to voicemail. The customer hangs up and calls the next contractor on Google. 
Every call is answered on the first ring, 24/7. The AI qualifies the customer, books the job directly into your CRM based on your capacity planning, and the lead never touches voicemail. Your marketing dollars actually convert into revenue.
Lead follow-up
A web form comes in from Angi or Google LSA. It sits in an inbox for 4-6 hours until someone gets to it. By then, the homeowner had already booked with whoever called them back first. The industry average response time is 30 hours; 90% of leads are gone after 20 seconds of waiting.
The moment a lead hits - web form, LSA, Thumbtack, etc - Avoca calls them within seconds, qualifies the job, and books it on your board. If they don't pick up, a voicemail and a text go out immediately, and a multi-touch cadence keeps working the lead until it converts or is exhausted.
Nurture campaigns
You have thousands of unsold estimates and lapsed memberships sitting in ServiceTitan. Your CSRs are too busy answering inbound calls to work outbound lists. You run a manual campaign once a quarter, maybe, and it dies after the first follow-up because nobody has time for touch #2 through #6.
AI runs proactive outbound campaigns — membership renewals, maintenance reminders, unsold estimate follow-up — automatically. Two-text SMS drips book at 6-15% rates. One Avoca customer generated $800K in revenue from a single branch's outbound campaign.
Scheduling
Your PPC landing page has a "Request a Quote" form. The customer fills it out, waits for a callback, and may or may not still be available by the time your team gets to it. Your cost per click is high, and your conversion rate is low because there's friction between the click and the booked job.
A scheduling widget on your landing page lets customers self-book into your live availability — no phone call, no wait. PPC conversion rates go up, cost per booked appointment goes down, and your board fills without a CSR touching it.
After hours and overflow
After 5 pm, calls go to an answering service that takes a message and emails it to you. The customer with the burst pipe at 10 pm gets told, "Someone will call you back in the morning." They hang up and call the next plumber. Your on-call tech never gets dispatched, and you lose a $1,500 emergency job to a competitor — plus that customer's lifetime value.
The AI answers after-hours calls identically to your daytime experience — same qualification, same empathy, same direct booking into your CRM. Emergency calls get dispatched immediately. If the call becomes complex or the customer wants a human, it is transferred to a live, Avoca-trained CSR. You wake up to jobs on your board, not voicemails to return.
