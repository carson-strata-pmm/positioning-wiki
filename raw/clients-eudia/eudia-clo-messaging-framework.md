Company Narrative & Positioning

POV: 
The Problem
How can legal move fast enough to continue effectively mitigating risk in a world where the speed of business is accelerating rapidly, and legacy solutions come with increasingly painful trade-offs?

Legal sits between two conflicting demands: operating at the speed of the business and protecting it. Move too slow, and you’re a drag on growth. Allow too much risk, the business is vulnerable. Historically, in-house teams, software, and external counsel have worked together to balance a carefully maintained speed/risk ratio. 

However, that ratio has become unsustainable in today's accelerating business environment. Growth, competition, and technology have dramatically changed the pace and predictability of operations. Risk is spread across your business, with portions of the knowledge spread across more and more individuals. Matter volume and complexity are escalating. But broadly compromising on risk tolerance isn’t an option. This leaves highly skilled and expensive in-house teams overloaded, under-informed, and reactive. CLOs naturally turn to outside firms to increase capacity and/or plug gaps in expertise, but they’re met with skyrocketing costs, transactional engagements, and a misalignment of priorities. Reason being, traditional law firms cling to outdated billable-hour models and manual processes, offering little innovation and questionable return on investment.

Legal leaders must rethink old solutions to not only maintain velocity and vigilance but also to transcend the speed/risk balancing act entirely to deliver new business value. 

What separates tomorrow’s successful Chief Legal Officers from a legacy general counsel is understanding that the purpose of the legal team is to enable the business to grow, not simply act as a risk manager or cost center. The CLO of the future recognizes that the legal team is sitting on a trove of valuable data that can both help the company grow while navigating risk by delivering actionable insights to the business. We’ve seen this evolution before: the CFO—once a cost center leader—now serves as the CEO’s right hand with financial insight ready.

Complicating this transformation is the fact that legacy legal software simply hasn’t been adopted by most lawyers, putting legal departments a generation behind in the requisite technology adoption journey. Other enterprise software categories (and the departments that use them: Finance, HR, Operations, and so forth) have radically changed behavior and gained a meaningful share of the departmental budget; legal technology has not. Compounding it all is the continued reliance on traditional law firms, which have refused to evolve, not because they can’t, but because their profitability depends on the system staying precisely as it is.
The Solution
At a high level, visionary legal leaders recognize that AI is the surest bet to help them meet the moment. It has the potential to do more than streamline some routine components of legal work and lessen the need for external firms—it can aggregate and analyze organizational knowledge and surface powerful insights that could redefine legal’s business role. But in practice, off-the-shelf Artificial Intelligence isn't fit for legal: it’s a minefield of risk and inaccuracy when it’s fed by public data (as opposed to proprietary), or requires extreme human oversight.

As a result, the vanguard of legal leaders is challenged to confidently translate the potential of nascent technology into reality. 

What legal needs is a platform that combines the best of AI with the best of what humans do—a brain that is a natural extension of the human team, supercharging productivity, eliminating routine work and illuminating previously unseen insights. A brain that aggregates knowledge across legal within a unique business context to navigate risk faster and better as a result. Equally important is a platform provider capable of designing and deploying such a solution efficiently and effectively, grounded in a deep understanding of a legal team’s technology adoption journey and their need for domain-specific 
knowledge and unwavering accuracy. 
But augmenting your in-house team alone is not enough. Most of your legal work will continue to flow through outside counsel, and if they aren’t evolving with you, they’re holding you back. You need to complement your team with Augmented Law Firms. Law firms that are completely reimagined and built from the ground up to align with your business through technology, not tradition. They don’t just deliver legal advice; they embed themselves in your business, know how to work with your legal brain, and provide faster, more competent guidance that aligns with your goals. Only together can you unlock a dramatically better way to protect the interests of your business and transcend the speed/risk ratio.
Meanwhile, those CLOs who fail to lean in and take charge of this pivotal moment will get away with continuing the status quo until another business function takes it upon themselves to transform legal as part of a broader back-office initiative, relegating the CLO to a ministerial General Counsel role.
Positioning
Eudia believes in a future where legal owns an elevated business charter, proactively steering the business toward opportunity while more efficiently mitigating associated risks. The best legal teams will transcend the speed/risk ratio and work 10x faster, mitigate risk 10x better, and deliver 100x more business value. 

This vision requires augmented legal teams—that is, a team of lawyers supercharged by AI harnessing the organizational data, institutional knowledge, and a cohesive understanding of risk that is unique to their context. This team of legal professionals can not only scale more efficiently by offloading mundane tasks and insight gathering, but also make better decisions with their shared “brain” of knowledge. In this future, the Chief Legal Officer is a strategic partner to the CEO and board, personally responsible for capturing billions in unrealized business value.

Only the Eudia Augmented Intelligence Platform makes this possible; it’s a “legal brain” that acts as an extension of your people, built with end-to-end workflows bespoke to your organization and unique use cases. The augmented human approach—your organization supercharged by the full breadth of your institutional knowledge—sets our platform apart from artificial intelligence. We deliver on the vision by putting a human expert in the loop to listen, advise, and capture your practices and processes. This allows us to earn the trust of your team, confidently bring the right AI solution for your business online faster than any alternative, grow your use cases over time to capture more value, and ensure exacting standards are upheld long-term.

→
AI-drafted contracts eliminate some of the basic back-and-forth, but still require a heavy burden of legal review to ensure the template and input are accurate, up-to-date, and adhere to the proper business context.
v.

Commercial Contract Risk Review & Identification
Lawyers engage in manual line-by-line reading, which can be extremely time consuming and tedious, especially for complex contracts.
→
AI alone will be limited by inconsistent CMS usage, and one-size-fits-all deployment may introduce new risk without significant oversight. It also lacks the ability to intelligently guide humans based on the overall risk portfolio.
v.
Significantly reduce the burden of review by automatically identifying risk within contracts based on the complete history of your contract library and recent activity, alongside potential steps for mitigation.
Commercial Contract Redlining
Lawyers engage in time-consuming manual line-by-line review; playbooks are often applied inconsistently on each review.
→
Similar to the above—one-size-fits-all deployment and limited input to the overall model create huge potential for risky or unusable AI output.
v.
Redline contracts based on risk identified, using specific language tailored to your legal department. Create consistent language across all your contracts vs. individualized language.
Commercial Due Diligence
Similar to the above: manual, painstaking processes, but often on a tight timeline that increases pressure on the legal team or the deal.
→
Off-the-shelf AI is unlikely to give humans in these extremely context-specific scenarios much utility (eg: previous M&A precedent, consolidated risk portfolio input, etc.)
v.
Close deals faster and with less external involvement. Make informed risk decisions in a fraction of the time on time-sensitive M&A matters.
Compliance & Regulation
Lawyers manually review certain materials, including public-facing materials and filings to ensure they are accurate, limit liability, and adhere to relevant rules and regulations.
→
While off-the-shelf AI may have the ability to broadly review materials for compliance issues, without bespoke knowledge of how a business approaches compliance risk, detailed manual review will still be necessary.
v.
Review more quickly and ensure tighter, more consistent adherence to specific rules, regulations, and protective guidance.
Privacy Transformation
A large team of lawyers review customer contracts to log customer data restrictions per contract. Output, which is usually logged in forms or excel, is hard to leverage at scale by business teams.
→
AI reviews contracts based on a data privacy guidance playbook. Some risks are flagged, some are not, requiring a human to review each contract anyway. 
v.
Ingest all past contracts and data privacy flags into the platform. This, alongside institutional knowledge captured from in-house experts, results in highly accurate output across a huge volume of contracts. Enable lawyers to focus on reviewing specific contracts that need attention.
Structured Insights (with Q&A, Search, and Citations)

→

Without bespoke deployment, any such offering would be rife with hallucination, inaccuracy, or limitation, and/or require significant manual oversight.
v.
Answer any question about past contracts in a quick search. Create an easy-to-search, consistent database of contract language, empowering legal to find information faster and be better informed about future contracting.
