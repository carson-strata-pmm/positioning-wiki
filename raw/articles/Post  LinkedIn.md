People overcomplicate positioning. There are really just three strategies... Strategy 1: Position in a mature category against existing vendors You place yourself in an established category and argue why you're better than the competition. The upside: Customers already have budget for it, actively search for it, and don't need educating. The risk: Mature categories have big, entrenched players. Without serious differentiation and resources, you'll struggle to make the shortlist. Choose this if... • the category is large enough to meet your growth goals • you have the resources to compete head-to-head for the whole market • OR you're willing to focus an underserved subsegment Strategy 2: Take an existing (but small) category to a new audience You position in a known (but likely nascent) category and sell it to people who haven't heard of it yet. (E.g. Skrillex bringing Dubstep to the mainstream. DocuSign didn't invent e-signature, but they focused on selling it to companies that used paper. Dropbox didn't invent file sharing, but they popularized it.) The upside: • Less direct competition, more greenfield opportunity The risk: • You have to educate the market rather than waiting for inbound demand • You also inherit the category's reputation (for good or bad) Choose this if... • you believe the category will grow and you can become a leader as it solidifies • you have the marketing patience to play the long game Strategy 3: Create your own category You refuse to fit into anyone else's box and seek to build a new one. The upside: You get full control to define both the problem and solution framing. The risks: • you need to pick the right-sized problem (too broad and it's unbelievable) • first movers rarely win (Airbnb beat Vrbo, Google beat AltaVista), and large incumbents may simply copy your blueprint once you've proven the market, as Microsoft did to Slack. Choose this if... • you genuinely believe any existing category label would hurt more than help • you're prepared to invest years building awareness from scratch If you need help with your positioning, shoot me a DM and mention this post.![View image](https://media.licdn.com/dms/image/v2/D5622AQG-gudJ1TE9LQ/feedshare-shrink_1280/B56Z6UG_2XJwAM-/0/1780601323382?e=1782345600&v=beta&t=qew6a2UIDJwqRWsoYbIjhT8Xf5W1feZxJRGl_x51Xks)
