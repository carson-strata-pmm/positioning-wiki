# Unity Games

## Company Overview
Unity for Games is the game development platform within Unity Technologies. Provides engine, tools, ad network, and monetization infrastructure for game developers across mobile, PC, console, and XR. The largest game development platform by adoption.

## Engagement Summary
Strata delivered a Platform Positioning & Messaging Framework. The framework addressed three distinct audiences (developers, UA/advertisers, monetization/publishers). Authors: Carson, Kevin. Unity contributors: Will, Ryan, Michael, Felipe, Jessica.

**Deliverables:**
- Platform Positioning & Messaging Framework

## Hero Problem
**"The Success Gap."** It's never been a better time to make games, but making a successful game now requires more than ever. Finding, retaining, and monetizing an audience across multiple platforms takes developers away from the creative work that matters most -- the game.

An earlier framing called it "The Infinite Grind" (surfaced in Jessica Lindl interview).

## POV / Opening Strategy
Positions the tension between creative ambition and operational overhead. The developer wants to make great games, but platform fragmentation, live ops demands, and opaque discovery/monetization systems pull them away from creative work. Unity's role is to "bridge that gap between ambition and success by abstracting the complexities."

## ICP
- **Developers:** Lead Game Developer, Lead Technical Director, Generalist/Indie dev -- value reliability, iteration speed, staying in creative flow
- **User Acquisition (Advertisers):** CMO, VP Growth, Head of UA -- finding right players at right cost in post-IDFA world
- **Monetization (Publishers):** Head of Monetization, VP Growth -- turning engagement into sustainable revenue across platforms

Note: "Many modern studios combine UA + Monetization under 'Growth.'"

## Category Positioning
Head-to-head competitor on two axes:
1. **vs. 3D editors (Unreal):** Complete ecosystem beyond engine (deploy + grow), lower barrier to entry (C# vs. C++), flexible philosophy (additive vs. subtractive), largest developer community
2. **vs. ad networks (AppLovin):** Full-stack (create + live ops + data), more control over game success, avoids platform lock-in

## Key Messaging Pillars
1. **Develop games without compromise** -- flexible toolset, production-verified engine, player insights from day one
2. **Deploy and run games across every platform without friction** -- build once ship everywhere, day-one platform support, built-in diagnostics
3. **Grow on your terms** -- intelligent discovery, freedom to monetize across platforms, actionable performance signals

## Proof Point Types Used
- **No named customer metrics or case studies** in the framework
- **Capability claims:** Production-verified engine, 1000s of device support, AI-driven player signals
- **Use cases (before/after):** Developing, deploying, and growing a game
- **Community proof:** "Largest game development community in the world," "relied on by thousands of studios"

## Lessons & Patterns
- **Multi-audience framework.** Three distinct buyer personas (developers, UA, monetization) with different key messages. The framework includes persona-specific language within a unified platform story.
- **"Develop, deploy, grow" is a sticky framework.** Jessica Lindl confirmed it was already found across internal operational plans before Strata formalized it, indicating strong internal alignment.
- **The "infinite grind" to "success gap" evolution** shows how hero problem naming is refined through collaboration. "Infinite grind" was more developer-centric; "success gap" is broader and more aspirational.
- **Technical differentiation is deep.** The Unreal comparison covers architecture philosophy (additive vs. subtractive), scripting language (C# vs. C++), and AI code generation readiness.
- **Ad network as competitor** is an unusual competitive frame -- Unity competes not just with other engines but with the monetization platforms that lock developers into specific business models.
