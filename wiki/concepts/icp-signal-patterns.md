# ICP Signal Patterns

## Definition

ICP (Ideal Customer Profile) signal patterns are the recurring characteristics, trigger events, and buyer archetypes that Strata identifies during engagements to define who the messaging should target. Strong ICP definition ensures messaging speaks to a specific person with a specific problem, not a generic market.

## How It Works

1. **Identify the primary buyer and their buying role** (decision maker, champion, influencer).
2. **Map trigger events** that create urgency: rapid growth, churn spikes, market shifts, technology disruption, PE acquisition, new executive hire.
3. **Segment by company stage or vertical** where the pain is most acute.
4. **Capture pain language** from interviews to validate that the ICP actually describes the problem the way the messaging does.

## Buyer Archetype Taxonomy

Across 11 client engagements, six recurring buyer archetypes emerge. Each archetype has distinct characteristics that shape how messaging should address them.

### 1. The Owner-Operator

**Who they are:** Solo or small-business leaders who are simultaneously the executive, the manager, and the doer. Business pain and personal pain are inseparable.

**Messaging implications:**
- Use second-person address ("your growth," "you feel overwhelmed")
- Empathetic, non-corporate language -- they respond to warmth, not jargon
- The hero problem should feel personal, not systemic
- Support and human touch are buying criteria, not just features

| Client | Buyer | Language Style |
|--------|-------|---------------|
| Avoca | Owner-Operator (CEO, GM, President) of home services businesses | "When something breaks, customers don't wait" -- direct, operational |
| Jane | Clinic Owner-Practitioner (solo decision maker) | "You're not just the clinician -- you're the admin, the owner, the systems department" -- empathetic, identity-level |

### 2. The Functional VP

**Who they are:** Mid-to-senior leaders who own a specific function (Finance, Legal, Marketing, Engineering). They have budget authority and feel the pain operationally but think strategically.

**Messaging implications:**
- Use third-person framing ("the [function] leader faces...")
- Lead with operational metrics and hidden costs
- Show the cross-functional impact (their pain affects other teams)
- Differentiation should address their specific tool landscape

| Client | Buyer | Function |
|--------|-------|----------|
| TeamOhana | VP of Finance / Head of FP&A | Finance -- controls budget, needs accurate data |
| BrightEdge | SEO/Organic Marketing Lead | Marketing -- managing hundreds of keyword dimensions |
| General Legal | General Counsel / Head of Legal | Legal -- mounting demand, flat resources |
| Eudia | CLO / Head of Legal | Legal -- speed-to-risk tension |
| Unity Industry | Head of Manufacturing, Head of Product Dev | Ops/R&D -- throughput, innovation speed |

### 3. The C-Suite Buyer

**Who they are:** CEO, CRO, CCO, or CFO. They buy based on strategic impact, not operational improvement. They need the "so what?" not the "how."

**Messaging implications:**
- Lead with market shift or inevitable future framing
- Quantify in terms of revenue, growth rate, competitive position
- The hero problem should feel like a strategic threat, not an operational annoyance
- Proof points must be executive-credible (named companies, board-level metrics)

| Client | Buyer | Strategic Frame |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Agency | CEO of AI-native startups | Fears losing customer intimacy at scale |
| Agency | CRO (mid-market) | Revenue leakage, operational leverage, margin preservation |
| General Legal | Founder/CEO (growth stage) | Needs legal guidance but can't justify in-house or traditional firm |
| Numeric | CFO (secondary) | Needs financial intelligence and real-time visibility |

### 4. The Technical Practitioner

**Who they are:** Developers, engineers, or technical specialists who evaluate based on how the product works, not just what it promises. They are skeptical of marketing language.

**Messaging implications:**
- Lead with mechanism, not outcomes ("how it works" before "what you get")
- Technical differentiation matters (architecture, language, integrations)
- Community proof and peer adoption carry more weight than logos
- Respect their expertise -- don't oversimplify

| Client | Buyer | Technical Frame |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Unity Games | Lead Game Developer, Lead Technical Director | Reliability, iteration speed, creative flow |
| Unity Games | CMO/VP Growth, Head of UA | Post-IDFA challenges, player acquisition cost |

### 5. The Cross-Functional Pair

**Who they are:** Two buyer roles from different functions who must both adopt the product for it to work. The messaging must serve both without over-indexing on either.

**Messaging implications:**
- Name both roles explicitly in the ICP section
- Show how the product bridges their different priorities
- The hero problem should name the gap *between* the two functions
- Differentiation should address why existing tools serve one function but not the other

| Client | Buyer A | Buyer B | Gap Between |
|--------|---------|---------|-------------|
| TeamOhana | VP Finance / FP&A | Head of Talent / Recruiting | Finance needs data; Talent needs workflow |
| Eudia | CLO (demand side) | Law Firm Partner (supply side) | CLO needs speed; Partner needs career stability |
| Numeric | Controller | CFO | Controller has data without strategic tools; CFO has mandate without operational detail |

### 6. The Transformation Leader

**Who they are:** CDO, VP of Digital Transformation, or CTO brought in to modernize. They're evaluating platforms, not point solutions. They buy vision and scalability.

**Messaging implications:**
- Lead with platform narrative and long-term vision
- Show how the product scales across the organization
- PE-backed portfolio buyers care about standardization across entities
- ROI must be expressed in terms of transformation metrics, not operational savings

| Client | Buyer | Transformation Frame |
|--------|-------|---------------------|
| Avoca | Digital Transformation Lead (CTO) | PE-backed portfolios standardizing tech across multi-location brands |
| Unity Industry | CDO, VP of Digital Transformation | Scaling digital tools across the enterprise |

## Trigger Event Taxonomy

Trigger events create urgency. The best messaging connects to a moment of acute pain, not a chronic condition. Across the corpus, six trigger categories emerge:

### Growth Triggers
Events where rapid growth outpaces existing processes or tools.

| Trigger | Client | Signal |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Headcount scaling | TeamOhana | Every new hire amplifies spreadsheet chaos |
| Revenue growth hitting capacity limits | Avoca | Call volume exceeds CSR staffing |
| Pre-IPO / post-IPO readiness | Numeric | Accounting processes can't meet public-company scrutiny |
| Rapid growth outpacing accounting | Numeric | Monthly close takes too long for decision-making pace |

### Workforce Triggers
Events where workforce dynamics create structural pain.

| Trigger | Client | Signal |
|---------|--------|--------|
| CSR churn cycle | Avoca | Every departure resets the customer experience (~$46K impact) |
| Seasonal staffing spikes | Avoca | Summer and winter demand surges overwhelm call centers |
| Legal team demand surge | Eudia | Matter volume up 79% with flat budgets |

### Technology Triggers
Events where technology shifts create new competitive threats.

| Trigger | Client | Signal |
|---------|--------|--------|
| AI disruption of search | BrightEdge | SGE and generative AI changing content discovery |
| Platform fragmentation | Unity Games | Multi-platform launches require stitching disconnected tools |
| AI collapse of content moat | BrightEdge | Generative AI levels the content creation playing field |

### Organizational Triggers
Events where internal changes create urgency.

| Trigger | Client | Signal |
|---------|--------|--------|
| New executive hire (CFO, Controller) | Numeric | New leader demands better data and processes |
| PE acquisition | Avoca | Portfolio standardization across locations |
| Cross-functional misalignment | TeamOhana | Finance and Talent operating with different tools and priorities |

### Market Triggers
Events where market dynamics create existential pressure.

| Trigger | Client | Signal |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Category disruption by AI | Agency, General Legal | AI-native competitors entering with fundamentally different models |
| Law firm partner career threat | Eudia (ALF) | The system partners sacrificed for has an expiration date |

### Lifecycle Triggers
Events where the buyer has outgrown their current approach.

| Trigger | Client | Signal |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Outgrown ad hoc legal | General Legal | Too much work for no legal team, not enough for traditional firm |
| Outgrown spreadsheets | TeamOhana | Spreadsheet headcount tracking breaks at ~100 employees |
| Outgrown patchwork tools | Jane | Gmail + Zoom + Calendly stack can't scale with practice growth |

## Stage-Based Segmentation Guide

Stage-based segmentation often matters more than industry vertical. Several clients segment their ICP by company maturity rather than sector.

### The Stage Spectrum

| Stage | Characteristics | Buying Behavior | Best Clients |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|--------------|
| **Early (Seed-Series A)** | <50 employees, founder-led, no dedicated function for the problem | Founder buys; price-sensitive; needs immediate ROI | General Legal (Growth segment), Jane |
| **Growth (Series B-C)** | 50-500 employees, function exists but processes are breaking | Functional VP buys; needs to replace manual processes | TeamOhana, Numeric, Avoca |
| **Scale (Series D+/pre-IPO)** | 500+ employees, multiple stakeholders, compliance pressure | Cross-functional buying committee; needs enterprise features | General Legal (Scale segment), Eudia |
| **Enterprise (public/PE)** | 1000+ employees, established processes, transformation-driven | Transformation leader or C-suite sponsors; long sales cycles | Unity Industry, Unity Games, BrightEdge |

### When to Use Stage vs. Vertical Segmentation

**Use stage-based segmentation when:**
- The pain is universal across industries but varies by company size (TeamOhana, General Legal)
- The trigger event is growth-related (Numeric, Avoca)
- The buying persona changes at different stages (General Legal: Founder at Growth, GC at Scale)

**Use vertical segmentation when:**
- The pain is industry-specific (Unity Industry: manufacturing; Eudia: legal)
- Regulatory or compliance requirements differ by vertical
- The competitive landscape is vertical-specific

**Use both when:**
- You need to prioritize within a vertical by company maturity
- Different verticals have different adoption timelines

## Multi-Buyer Engagement Patterns

Most B2B deals involve at least two buyer personas. The corpus shows three patterns for handling multi-buyer dynamics:

### Pattern 1: Primary + Secondary (same narrative, different emphasis)
One messaging framework with persona-specific emphasis sections. Most common pattern.

> **Avoca:** Primary (Owner-Operator) + Secondary (Customer Service Leader) + Tertiary (CTO). One framework, but language shifts from empathetic (owner) to operational (CS leader) to strategic (CTO).

### Pattern 2: Dual narrative (separate frameworks)
Two distinct messaging frameworks for two distinct audiences. Used when the audiences have fundamentally different relationships to the product.

> **Eudia:** CLO narrative (demand side) and ALF narrative (supply side). Separate hero problems, separate pillars, separate proof. Connected by a Messaging Map.

### Pattern 3: Multi-persona framework (one framework, multiple persona sections)
One framework with dedicated sections per persona within the value pillars.

> **Unity Games:** Developers, UA/Advertisers, and Monetization/Publishers. Single platform story ("develop, deploy, grow") with persona-specific language within each pillar.

## Patterns & Lessons

- **Owner-operators are a unique archetype.** Avoca and Jane both target owner-operators who wear every hat. Messaging for this persona must be empathetic, not corporate. Second-person address is required.
- **Stage-based segmentation matters more than industry.** General Legal segments by "Scale" vs. "Growth" stage, not by vertical. TeamOhana maps to growth phase. This is often cleaner.
- **The "long tail" opportunity is underappreciated.** Agency targets the 80-90% of enterprise customers that legacy teams ignore. This is a powerful framing for AI-first products.
- **Trigger events create urgency.** The best messaging connects to a moment of acute pain. Chronic conditions don't drive urgency -- triggers do.
- **Cross-functional buyers need explicit bridging.** TeamOhana, Eudia, and Numeric all have cross-functional buyer pairs. The hero problem must name the gap between the two functions.
- **Academic/skeptical buyers need different language.** Eudia's framework explicitly defines voice as "academic, insider, inventive" because lawyers are uniquely skeptical. Generic SaaS copy fails with this audience.
