# Homepage Review Playbook

A step-by-step guide for evaluating and improving a B2B software homepage's positioning. Derived from analysis of 7 Fletch before/after homepage repositioning examples and the Strata messaging framework.

---

## Situation

Use this playbook when:
- A client's homepage isn't converting and needs repositioning
- You're reviewing a new client's homepage during discovery
- A messaging framework has been delivered and the homepage needs to express it
- A founder asks "what's wrong with our homepage?"

The homepage is where positioning claims are tested in seconds. It's the most compressed expression of the full messaging hierarchy.

## Prerequisites

1. **Access to the current homepage** (live URL or screenshot)
2. **Clarity on the target buyer** -- you need to know who should be reading this page
3. **Ideally, a completed messaging framework** -- but this playbook can also be used diagnostically before framework work begins

## Step-by-Step Sequence

### Step 1: Score the homepage on 7 dimensions

Use the Homepage Positioning Rubric. Score each dimension 1-3:

| Dimension | 1 (Weak) | 2 (Adequate) | 3 (Strong) |
|-----------|----------|--------------|------------|
| **Problem Clarity** | No named problem, or generic/abstract | Problem exists but isn't sharp or buyer-specific | Specific, named problem that a clear buyer owns |
| **Category Anchor** | No category context; visitor can't place the product | Category implied but not stated | Explicit category with clear positioning within it |
| **Product Explanation** | Unclear what the product does | Understandable but abstract or benefit-heavy | Concrete, plain-language explanation of mechanism |
| **Differentiation** | None stated or only vague superlatives | Some differentiation but generic ("AI-powered") | Specific, targeted differentiation vs. alternatives |
| **Point of View** | Descriptive only; no market stance | Mild opinion but doesn't challenge status quo | Strong, opinionated stance on what's broken |
| **Proof & Credibility** | No social proof or metrics | Logos or generic testimonials | Specific metrics, named customers, concrete results |
| **Page Architecture** | Too thin or too sprawling; no clear flow | Has sections but sequence is unclear | Clear hero → problem → solution → proof → CTA flow |

**Scoring guide:**
- **18-21:** Strong positioning -- ready for market
- **13-17:** Functional but has gaps that reduce conversion
- **7-12:** Needs repositioning work

**Decision point:** If the total score is 18+, the homepage likely needs copy refinement, not repositioning. If 12 or below, structural repositioning is needed.

### Step 2: Identify which dimensions are weakest

Map the lowest-scoring dimensions to the six strategic moves that fix them:

| Weak Dimension | Strategic Move | What to Do |
|---------------|---------------|------------|
| Problem Clarity (1-2) | **Problem Ownership** | Rewrite the hero section so a specific buyer role recognizes the problem as theirs |
| Category Anchor (1-2) | **Category Anchoring** | Add an explicit category label or positioning line |
| Product Explanation (1-2) | **Concrete Explanation** | Replace abstract benefits with plain-language description of what the product does |
| Differentiation (1-2) | **Explicit Differentiation** | Name specific ways the product is different from alternatives |
| Point of View (1-2) | **Opinionated Stance** | Add a position on what's broken in the market |
| Proof & Credibility (1-2) | **Proof Addition** | Add metrics, logos, quotes, or case study references |
| Page Architecture (1-2) | **Architecture Restructuring** | Expand (if too thin) or compress (if too sprawling) |

**Decision point:** Fix no more than 3 dimensions at once. If all 7 are weak, this is a full repositioning project, not a homepage tweak.

### Step 3: Check the Universal Positioning Formula

The homepage should follow this sequence. Identify which steps are missing:

```
1. OPENING: Name the problem or market shift (hero section)
   → "Why should I care?"

2. ANCHOR: State the category or product type
   → "What kind of thing is this?"

3. EXPLAIN: Describe what the product does, concretely
   → "What does it actually do?"

4. DIFFERENTIATE: Name specific ways it's different
   → "Why this vs. alternatives?"

5. PROVE: Show evidence (logos, metrics, quotes)
   → "Should I believe you?"

6. CONVERT: Clear CTA matched to buyer intent
   → "What do I do next?"
```

> **The critical insight from the Fletch corpus:** Weak homepages skip steps 1, 2, or 4. They jump to features (step 3) without the problem (step 1), the category (step 2), or differentiation (step 4).

### Step 4: Check for the 7 most common before mistakes

Run through this checklist. Each "yes" is a problem to fix:

| # | Mistake | Check |
|---|---------|-------|
| 1 | **No anchoring element** -- use cases listed without a category frame | Can a visitor tell what *kind* of product this is within 5 seconds? |
| 2 | **No problem owner** -- the problem is real but nobody specific owns it | Would a person in the target buyer role say "that's my problem"? |
| 3 | **Abstract benefits instead of concrete use cases** -- "unlock growth" without specifics | Could a visitor describe what the product *does* after reading the hero? |
| 4 | **No differentiation** -- describes the product without saying why it's different | Does the page answer "why you vs. alternatives"? |
| 5 | **No point of view** -- descriptive but not opinionated | Does the company take a stance on what's broken in the market? |
| 6 | **Unclear what the product does** -- too focused on vision or outcomes | Could a visitor explain the product to a colleague? |
| 7 | **Page depth mismatch** -- too thin (no substance) or too long (feature sprawl) | Does the page length match the complexity of the positioning? |

### Step 5: Rewrite recommendations

For each identified problem, write a specific recommendation. Use this format:

```
DIMENSION: [which rubric dimension]
CURRENT: [what the homepage currently says/does]
PROBLEM: [which mistake pattern it matches]
RECOMMENDATION: [specific change, with example language if possible]
STRATEGIC MOVE: [which of the 6 moves this applies]
```

**Decision point:** If the homepage has a messaging framework behind it, recommendations should pull from the framework. If no framework exists, recommendations should flag that framework work is needed before homepage copy can be finalized.

### Step 6: Validate page architecture direction

Based on the Fletch corpus patterns, determine if the page needs to get longer or shorter:

| Current State | Direction | Example |
|--------------|-----------|---------|
| **Hero only, no supporting structure** | Expand -- add problem section, solution section, proof | Concierge AI: expanded from ~550px to ~3076px |
| **Feature sprawl without focused positioning** | Compress -- cut non-essential sections, sharpen focus | Omnibound: compressed from ~6401px to ~5436px |
| **Right length, wrong sequence** | Restructure -- reorder sections to follow the Universal Formula | Most common pattern |

**The fundamental trade-off:** Weak pages are almost always too wide (too many use cases, too many buyers, too many features). Strong pages are narrower but deeper (focused on one buyer, one problem, one category -- but proving it thoroughly). **Breadth feels safer but converts worse.**

## Common Mistakes

1. **Reviewing copy without reviewing positioning.** Bad copy is usually a symptom of bad positioning. If the homepage doesn't know who it's for, what problem it solves, or how it's different, wordsmithing the headline won't help.

2. **Trying to address multiple buyers on one page.** The homepage should speak to the primary buyer. Secondary buyers get their own pages. Trying to serve everyone serves no one.

3. **Adding more content to a page that needs less.** When the problem is feature sprawl, the fix is subtraction, not addition. Cut sections that don't serve the primary buyer's decision-making journey.

4. **Treating the homepage as a product tour.** The homepage's job is positioning (why should I care, what is this, why you). The product tour is a separate page or experience.

5. **Skipping proof.** Early-stage companies often have no proof section. Even one customer metric, one logo, or one specific result is better than nothing. "Social proof desert" is a conversion killer.

## Examples from the Corpus

| Company | Primary Before Problem | Primary Fix | Score Change (estimated) |
|---------|----------------------|-------------|------------------------|
| CommandBar | No category anchor, no differentiation, no POV | Category anchoring + explicit differentiation + opinionated stance | ~9 → ~18 |
| LoneScale | No problem owner, abstract benefits, unclear product | Problem ownership + concrete explanation + buyer-resonance filtering | ~8 → ~17 |
| UserGems | Didn't describe problem, product, or differentiation | Full positioning clarity (all three core questions) | ~7 → ~16 |
| Concierge AI | Minimal page (hero only) | Page architecture expansion | ~6 → ~15 |
| Omnibound | Feature sprawl | Page compression + positioning focus | ~10 → ~16 |
