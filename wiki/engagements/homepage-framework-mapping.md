# Homepage Framework Mapping

A reverse-engineering exercise: mapping each of the 7 Fletch before/after homepage examples back to the Strata messaging framework structure. Only layers that can be cleanly inferred from available homepage text and annotations are mapped. Where the content is locked in screenshots without extractable text, layers are marked accordingly.

> Note: The Fletch before/after corpus is image-heavy. Most homepage content lives in screenshots, not extractable text. CommandBar and LoneScale have explicit text annotations; UserGems has a detailed description. The remaining four (Altura, DinMo, Concierge AI, Omnibound) have only category labels and page dimensions. Confidence levels reflect this constraint.

## 1. CommandBar

**Category:** Product Management Tech | **Product:** Digital Adoption Tools

| Framework Layer | What Was Found | Confidence |
|----------------|---------------|------------|
| **Hero Problem** | Implied: digital adoption is fragmented and ineffective. The after version takes an "opinionated stance" — suggesting the homepage names what's broken about current digital adoption tools. | Low |
| **POV / Opening Strategy** | Before: no strong POV. After: "highly opinionated stance" — likely an indictment of existing digital adoption approaches. | High (annotation confirms POV shift) |
| **Pain Pillars** | Not determinable from homepage alone. | Not determinable |
| **Value Pillars** | Before covered "a variety of use cases" without anchoring; after anchors use cases to the "digital adoption tools" category with targeted differentiation. Specific pillars not extractable. | Low |
| **Proof Points** | Not determinable from homepage alone. | Not determinable |
| **ICP** | Product managers or product teams responsible for user onboarding and in-app guidance. Inferred from "Product Management Tech" category label and "Nudges" product name. | Low |
| **Category Stance** | Category repositioning. Before: no category anchor. After: explicitly positioned within "digital adoption tools" with differentiation against the category. This is redefinition — entering an existing category with a distinct point of view. | High |

**Strategic intent:** CommandBar's repositioning is fundamentally a category anchoring play. The before homepage tried to be everything to everyone (multiple use cases, no frame). The after homepage makes a deliberate bet: we are a digital adoption tool, and here's why we're the opinionated one in this space. This mirrors the Strata pattern of choosing a category and then differentiating within it rather than avoiding categorization. The addition of a strong POV suggests the homepage now opens with a market stance — likely indicting how current digital adoption tools work — before introducing the product.

## 2. LoneScale

**Category:** Sales Intelligence | **Sector:** Sales Tech

| Framework Layer | What Was Found | Confidence |
|----------------|---------------|------------|
| **Hero Problem** | Before: a real problem existed but "no clear owner." After: refocused on "a use case someone owns and is actively trying to solve." | **POV / Opening Strategy** | Before: high-level benefits (abstract, aspirational). After: "simple, direct explanation of what the product does and how it works." The opening likely shifted from vision-first to problem-first. | Low (inferred from annotation pattern) |
| **Pain Pillars** | Not determinable from homepage alone. | Not determinable |
| **Value Pillars** | After version is "built around the part of the product that resonates most with real buyers." This implies value pillars were filtered down from a broad set to a focused set based on buyer feedback. Specific pillars not extractable. | Low |
| **Proof Points** | Not determinable from homepage alone. | Not determinable |
| **ICP** | Sales teams. After: messaging is matched to the people who own the problem. | High (description explicitly discusses buyer-message mismatch) |
| **Category Stance** | Not determinable. The repositioning was about problem ownership and concreteness, not category. | Not determinable |

**Strategic intent:** LoneScale's repositioning is a buyer-resonance filtering play. The before homepage had a feature-message mismatch: it showed the wrong capabilities to the wrong people. The strategic move was to identify which buyer actually felt the pain, which part of the product solved it, and rebuild the homepage around that intersection. This maps to the Strata principle that messaging must be built from buyer language and buyer workflows, not from the product's full capability set. The shift from "high-level benefits" to "simple, direct explanation" also suggests a move away from vision-led messaging toward mechanism-led messaging — showing how the product works, not just what outcomes it promises.

## Cross-Example Framework Mapping Summary

| Company | Layers Determinable | Layers Not Determinable | Dominant Framework Gap (Before) |
|---------|-------------------|------------------------|-------------------------------|
| CommandBar | POV, Category Stance | Hero Problem, Pain/Value Pillars, Proof, ICP | No POV, no category anchor |
| LoneScale | ICP (partial), Hero Problem (shift only) | Pain/Value Pillars, Proof, Category | No problem owner, wrong features highlighted |
| UserGems | None fully; shift confirmed | All specific layers | Baseline positioning incoherence |
| Altura | None | All | Insufficient data |
| DinMo | None | All | Insufficient data |
| Concierge AI | None; architecture shift confirmed | All | No page architecture (hero only) |
| Omnibound | None; architecture shift confirmed | All | Feature sprawl without focus |

**Key finding:** Even with limited textual data, the framework mapping reveals that homepage repositioning most often targets the **opening layers** of the Strata framework — Hero Problem, POV/Opening Strategy, and Category Stance. These are the layers that determine whether a visitor stays or bounces. Pain Pillars, Value Pillars, and Proof Points are supporting layers that matter for conversion depth but are rarely the primary repositioning target on a homepage.
